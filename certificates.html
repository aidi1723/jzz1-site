Why certificate requests often get delayed

  • The country is named, but the specific standard or building code edition is missing
  • The buyer requests compliance before the window system (sliding vs tilt-turn) is chosen
  • Glass thickness, interlayer, or hardware specifications are still fluctuating
  • The certifier needs Form 15 / Form 16 format, but only headline prices were shared
  • Mixed packages (e.g. windows, balustrades, gates) are grouped under one single standard

What speeds up compliance confirmation

  • Identify target standard: e.g. AS 2047-2014, AS/NZS 2208, or BCA SS 341
  • Share the architectural window schedule or elevation PDF
  • Specify wind region or design pressure (e.g. N3, ULS 3000 Pa, or coastal exposure)
  • State whether certifier requires test report copy, factory declaration, or sample unit
  • Confirm glass build-up early (e.g. 6mm Toughened + 12Ar + 6mm Toughened Low-E)

Frequently Asked Questions on International Certification

How does AS 2047 certification work for imported windows in Australia?

Under the National Construction Code (NCC), windows and glazed doors must comply with AS 2047. Rather than one-off test claims, JZZ1 holds StandardsMark Licence SMK41678 from Intertek SAI Global, which includes ongoing factory quality audits, full traceability, and verified NATA test reports for certified models like MD75S and MD76A.

Does JZZ1 provide AS/NZS 2208 glass stamps and certification?

Yes. Every piece of architectural safety glass installed in our export systems carries the permanent AS/NZS 2208 compliance bug/stamp detailing the standard, licence number, and glass type (toughened or laminated), backed by CSi Licence 7632.

What documentation is provided for Australian building surveyors?

We provide a complete documentation package for your project certifier: SAI Global StandardsMark Licence copy, CSi AS/NZS 2208 certificate, laboratory structural test report, glazing certificate, and manufacturer compliance declaration suitable for Form 15 preparation.
