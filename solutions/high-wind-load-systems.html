Executive Structural Engineering & Manufacturing Summary

In coastal hurricane belts (such as Florida and the Caribbean) and East Asian typhoon zones (such as the Philippines, Taiwan, and coastal Southeast Asia), building facades must resist severe cyclical wind gusts, violent flying debris impact, and torrential horizontal rain. Standard residential window profiles rack, leak, and risk glass blow-out under these extreme conditions.

At Jinan Jingzao New Materials Co., Ltd. (JZZ1), we engineer and manufacture certified high wind load window and door systems built on three structural pillars:

  • Structural Deflection Control (L/175 & L/240): Heavy-wall 6063-T6 aluminum profiles (2.0mm to 3.0mm wall thickness) reinforced with internal steel or high-inertia aluminum stiffeners (moment of inertia Ix ≥ 45–120 cm&sup4;) to limit lateral mullion deflection under ±3.0 kPa to 4.5 kPa pressure.
  • SGP & PVB Impact-Resistant Laminated Glazing: 1.52mm or 2.28mm SentryGlas (SGP) ionoplast interlayer glass that remains rigid and weather-sealed inside the frame even if both glass lites fracture during missile impact testing (ASTM E1886 / E1996).
  • Stepped Water-Dam Thresholds (600–700 Pa): Deep 55mm–75mm stepped sills with hidden drainage weep channels and spring-loaded baffle valves that prevent storm water backflow under continuous negative pressure.

Engineering Strengths: Multi-Point Compression vs Dynamic Suction

During hurricanes and typhoons, high-rise facades experience severe negative suction pressure on leeward elevations. Window sashes with weak latches get sucked outward, causing catastrophic seal blow-out:

  • Perimeter Mushroom-Head Locking Cams: Our casement and awning sashes engage heavy-duty 316 stainless steel mushroom cams into frame strike plates every 400mm–600mm around the perimeter, holding the sash clamped against vulcanized EPDM gaskets under negative wind suction.
  • Structural Silicone Glazing Bite: High-wind IGUs are structurally glazed using Dow Corning / Sika two-part neutral structural silicone with minimum 12mm–18mm contact bite, anchoring the glass pane mechanically to the aluminum frame.

Frequently Asked Questions: High Wind Load Aluminum Windows & Doors

What design wind pressure (Pa) can your reinforced aluminum windows withstand?

JZZ1 high wind load systems are engineered and tested to withstand serviceability wind pressures (SLS) of 1500 Pa to 2000 Pa and ultimate limit state (ULS) pressures of 3000 Pa to 4500 Pa (equivalent to Category 5 typhoon gusts exceeding 250 km/h) under ASTM E330 and AS 2047 testing standards.

How do you prevent water penetration during extreme typhoons?

We implement a 55mm to 75mm stepped high-low sill with an exterior drop-down drainage channel, concealed weep holes with silicone gravity flaps, and multi-stage vulcanized EPDM compression gaskets. This architecture achieves water tightness ratings of 600 Pa to 700 Pa under cyclic static and dynamic spray testing.

What glass build-up is recommended for coastal high wind pressure zones?

For coastal high-rise buildings and typhoon zones, we recommend laminated safety glass units such as 6mm Tempered + 1.52mm SGP (or PVB) + 6mm Tempered, or insulated laminated units (6mm Low-E + 12Ar + 6mm + 1.52PVB + 6mm). SGP (SentryGlas) is preferred for missile impact and high cyclic pressure retention.
